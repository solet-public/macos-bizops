## Approved statement

Principles, not rules. A rule is a past decision with its reason removed. No set of rules covers every case. A system that tries ends up with more rules than anyone can follow, with people citing rules instead of thinking, and with nobody answerable because "I followed the rule." So: understand the purpose, measure before you decide, decide, act, and say in one line which principle you applied and why. When you are about to write or cite "never", "must" or "per ruling", stop and state the purpose instead. You own the outcome, not compliance.
A lane on a small model gets a plan, not rules: exact steps for one task, written by a session that did the thinking, each with its purpose. When the steps do not fit what it finds, it stops and reports.
